# Bloomhaven Flower Pack: Yellowstone Wildflowers

This pack adds four Yellowstone National Park inspired flowers using the Bloomhaven Flower Creation Bible. These are content-ready profiles for design, illustration, and future implementation. They should not be added to gameplay until their art assets and balance values are reviewed in context.

## Pack Design Goals

- Add real wildflowers associated with Yellowstone National Park.
- Make each flower visually distinct at mobile scale.
- Support discovery, education, hybrid clues, customer requests, and journal entries.
- Keep the tone 80% grounded and 20% magical.
- Treat rare or sensitive plants respectfully as field-guide discoveries, not harvest targets from protected land.

## 1. Yellowstone Sand Verbena

### Full Botanical Profile

- Scientific Name: Abronia ammophila
- Common Name: Yellowstone Sand Verbena
- Flower Family: Nyctaginaceae, four-o'clock family
- Native Region: Endemic to Yellowstone National Park, especially sandy shores of Yellowstone Lake
- USDA Hardiness Zones: 3-6, design estimate for cold high-elevation gameplay use
- Bloom Season: July-August
- Sun Requirements: Full sun
- Water Requirements: Low to moderate; prefers fast-draining shoreline sand
- Soil Requirements: Loose sandy soil with excellent drainage
- Height: 2-6 inches, with spreading stems up to about 16 inches
- Width: 8-18 inches
- Pollinator Information: Supports small bees and moth-like evening pollinators in Bloomhaven fiction; use soft nighttime pollinator animation
- Interesting Historical Fact: Yellowstone sand verbena is one of the few plant species endemic to Yellowstone.
- Bloomhaven Lore Entry: "Grandfather wrote that the shore blooms did not belong to any ordinary garden. They were patient flowers, listening to warm sand beneath cold stars."

### Soil Temperament

- Primary Soil Temperament: Sandy
- Secondary Tolerance: Shoreline
- Avoid: Heavy wet clay

### Native Zone

- Bloomhaven Native Zone: Yellowstone Lake Shore
- Biome Tags: Shoreline, Sand, High Elevation, Endemic

### Growth Stages

1. Seedling: Two rounded green leaves emerging from pale sand, with one tiny pebble for scale.
2. Young Plant: Low mat of soft green leaves with a few crawling stems.
3. Bud Stage: Small clustered white buds held close to the sand.
4. Bloom: Rounded clusters of white star-tubed flowers with a pale cream center.

### Natural Color Varieties

- Lake Pearl: clean white petals with cream centers
- Sand Blush: white petals with faint pink-lavender edges
- Moonlit Cream: warm ivory petals with pale yellow centers

### Traits

- Primary Traits: Endemic, Shoreline, Delicate
- Secondary Traits: Sandy, Rare, Night-Scented
- Hidden Traits: Thermal Memory, Moonlit, Grandfather's Secret

### Flower Statistics

- Rarity: Rare
- Sell Value: 52 coins
- Growth Days: 5
- Fragrance: 3/5
- Beauty: 4/5
- Pollinator Appeal: 3/5
- Growth Difficulty: 4/5
- Hybrid Compatibility: 4/5

### Customer Appeal

- Loved By: Professor Finch, Millie Rabbit
- Customer Types: Collectors, researchers, restoration patrons
- Best Order Types: Collector Request, Botanical Society Study, Shoreline Bouquet
- Business Note: High reputation value, moderate coin value. This flower should feel precious rather than commercial.

### Pollinator Relationships

- Bee Appeal: Medium
- Butterfly Appeal: Low
- Moth Appeal: High
- Special Pollinator: Moon Moth
- Pollination Hook: Night or evening step events may reveal stronger clues when this flower is blooming.

### Seasonal Availability

- Primary Season: Summer
- Secondary Season: Late Summer
- Weather Synergy: Warm Evening
- Step Event Synergy: Ancient Journal Fragment, Rare Pollinator

### Educational Content

- Educational Fact: Endemic plants occur naturally in a limited geographic area and can be closely tied to specific habitats.
- Horticultural Tip: Sandy-soil flowers usually need drainage more than rich soil.
- Bloomhaven Journal Note: "Some flowers are rare because they are fragile. Some are rare because the world only made one small home for them."

### Illustration Deliverables

- Seedling: tiny green leaves in sand
- Young Plant: low spreading mat
- Bud Stage: clustered pale buds
- Mature Bloom: rounded white flower cluster
- Color Variants: Lake Pearl, Sand Blush, Moonlit Cream
- Journal Portrait: shoreline sand, blue lake hint, one flower cluster centered
- Inventory Icon: white cluster over tan sand badge
- Market Thumbnail: small seed packet with shoreline stripe
- Stained Glass Badge: pearl flower against blue lake arc

### Animation Standard

- Idle: gentle low sway, almost like breathing over sand
- Bloom: petals open with a faint moon-pollen sparkle
- Special: at night, one soft moth silhouette crosses behind the flower

### Approval Checklist

- Recognizable as a low white sand verbena cluster
- Clearly different from Daisy and Phlox
- Mobile-readable in a 48px icon
- Rare, delicate, and grounded
- Does not imply harvesting protected park plants in the real world

## 2. Silvery Lupine

### Full Botanical Profile

- Scientific Name: Lupinus argenteus
- Common Name: Silvery Lupine
- Flower Family: Fabaceae, pea family
- Native Region: Western North America, including Rocky Mountain meadow and sagebrush communities
- USDA Hardiness Zones: 3-8
- Bloom Season: June-August
- Sun Requirements: Full sun to partial sun
- Water Requirements: Low to moderate
- Soil Requirements: Well-drained meadow, sagebrush, or open forest soil
- Height: 8-28 inches
- Width: 8-18 inches
- Pollinator Information: Attractive to bees and butterflies; lupines also support some butterfly larvae.
- Interesting Historical Fact: The species name argenteus means silvery, referring to the plant's pale, silver-toned foliage.
- Bloomhaven Lore Entry: "The lupines looked like little blue towers along the old road. Grandfather said they were signposts for bees."

### Soil Temperament

- Primary Soil Temperament: Meadow
- Secondary Tolerance: Sandy Loam
- Avoid: Waterlogged soil

### Native Zone

- Bloomhaven Native Zone: Silver Meadow
- Biome Tags: Meadow, Wildflower, Pollinator, Nitrogen-Fixer

### Growth Stages

1. Seedling: Pair of small round leaves with a faint silver edge.
2. Young Plant: Palmate leaf fan with five to seven leaflets.
3. Bud Stage: Short upright spike with bead-like purple buds.
4. Bloom: Tall blue-purple pea-flower spire with silvery green leaves below.

### Natural Color Varieties

- Silver Blue: blue-violet flowers with pale banner spots
- Lavender Mist: softer lavender-purple flower spike
- White Banner: pale blue-white flowers with yellow-white centers

### Traits

- Primary Traits: Wild, Pollinator, Meadow
- Secondary Traits: Silvery, Spire, Hardy
- Hidden Traits: Bee Road, Soil Builder, Alpine Memory

### Flower Statistics

- Rarity: Uncommon
- Sell Value: 34 coins
- Growth Days: 4
- Fragrance: 1/5
- Beauty: 4/5
- Pollinator Appeal: 5/5
- Growth Difficulty: 2/5
- Hybrid Compatibility: 4/5

### Customer Appeal

- Loved By: Millie Rabbit, Professor Finch
- Customer Types: Native gardeners, meadow decorators, pollinator clubs
- Best Order Types: Meadow Bouquet, Pollinator Box, Wildflower Bundle
- Business Note: Reliable early-mid game flower that rewards players for building pollinator-focused gardens.

### Pollinator Relationships

- Bee Appeal: High
- Butterfly Appeal: High
- Moth Appeal: Low
- Special Pollinator: Blue Butterfly
- Pollination Hook: Adds a small bonus to Pollination generation when planted near other Wildflower family blooms.

### Seasonal Availability

- Primary Season: Summer
- Secondary Season: Late Spring
- Weather Synergy: Sunny
- Step Event Synergy: Bee Swarm, Migrating Butterflies

### Educational Content

- Educational Fact: Lupines are legumes, and many legumes can form relationships with soil bacteria that help cycle nitrogen.
- Horticultural Tip: Lupines usually prefer sunny, well-drained spaces and do not like sitting in soggy soil.
- Bloomhaven Journal Note: "The leaves hold silver even when the sky is gray. A useful trait, if beauty can also be useful."

### Illustration Deliverables

- Seedling: tiny silver-edged leaf pair
- Young Plant: clear palmate leaf silhouette
- Bud Stage: small upright bud tower
- Mature Bloom: blue-purple spire with pea-flower shapes
- Color Variants: Silver Blue, Lavender Mist, White Banner
- Journal Portrait: vertical spire, meadow grass base, bee trail dots
- Inventory Icon: compact purple spire
- Market Thumbnail: seed packet with silver leaf motif
- Stained Glass Badge: blue spire with silver-green leaf fan

### Animation Standard

- Idle: slow vertical sway
- Bloom: buds light from bottom to top as flowers open
- Special: bees pause on alternating blossoms in the spire

### Approval Checklist

- Reads as lupine, not lavender
- Strong vertical silhouette
- Silver foliage visible but not noisy
- Works as an uncommon pollinator flower
- Easy to distinguish at farm-plot scale

## 3. Scarlet Paintbrush

### Full Botanical Profile

- Scientific Name: Castilleja miniata
- Common Name: Scarlet Paintbrush
- Flower Family: Orobanchaceae, broomrape family
- Native Region: Western North America, including wet mountain meadows and streamside habitats
- USDA Hardiness Zones: 3-8
- Bloom Season: June-August in Yellowstone-style gameplay
- Sun Requirements: Full sun to partial sun
- Water Requirements: Moderate
- Soil Requirements: Moist meadow soil with neighboring plant roots nearby
- Height: 12-32 inches
- Width: 8-16 inches
- Pollinator Information: Bright bracts and tubular flowers are useful visual cues for hummingbirds, bees, and butterflies.
- Interesting Historical Fact: The most colorful parts of many paintbrush species are bracts, not petals.
- Bloomhaven Lore Entry: "Grandfather sketched these in red ink and wrote only: 'Not all flowers show their true color in the obvious place.'"

### Soil Temperament

- Primary Soil Temperament: Moist Meadow
- Secondary Tolerance: Streamside
- Avoid: Dry exposed sand

### Native Zone

- Bloomhaven Native Zone: Fire Meadow
- Biome Tags: Meadow, Streamside, Bract Flower, Companion

### Growth Stages

1. Seedling: Narrow green leaves with a small reddish stem tip.
2. Young Plant: Upright leafy shoots, simple and sturdy.
3. Bud Stage: Tight orange-red bracts forming a pointed brush shape.
4. Bloom: Scarlet-orange bract tower with small hidden yellow-green tubular flowers.

### Natural Color Varieties

- Scarlet Flame: vivid red-orange bracts
- Salmon Brush: softer coral-orange bracts
- Ember Rose: pink-red bracts with warm orange tips

### Traits

- Primary Traits: Bold, Meadow, Companion
- Secondary Traits: Scarlet, Bract, Hummingbird
- Hidden Traits: Borrowed Strength, Painted Secret, Flame Mark

### Flower Statistics

- Rarity: Uncommon
- Sell Value: 38 coins
- Growth Days: 4
- Fragrance: 1/5
- Beauty: 5/5
- Pollinator Appeal: 4/5
- Growth Difficulty: 4/5
- Hybrid Compatibility: 4/5

### Customer Appeal

- Loved By: Wendy Wren, Millie Rabbit
- Customer Types: Wedding planners, festival decorators, artists
- Best Order Types: Accent Bouquet, Festival Banner Flowers, Personal Request
- Business Note: Lower fragrance but very high visual drama. Orders should pay more when Beauty is requested.

### Pollinator Relationships

- Bee Appeal: Medium
- Butterfly Appeal: Medium
- Hummingbird Appeal: High
- Special Pollinator: Ruby Hummingbird
- Pollination Hook: Pairing with meadow flowers can reveal Companion trait clues.

### Seasonal Availability

- Primary Season: Summer
- Secondary Season: Late Summer
- Weather Synergy: Rainy or Cloudy
- Step Event Synergy: Rare Pollinator, Weather Forecast

### Educational Content

- Educational Fact: In some flowers, colorful bracts do the visual work that petals do in other species.
- Horticultural Tip: Some meadow plants depend on neighboring plants and soil communities, so they are harder to cultivate alone.
- Bloomhaven Journal Note: "A flower can be honest and still wear a disguise."

### Illustration Deliverables

- Seedling: narrow green leaves, faint red stem
- Young Plant: upright leafy plant
- Bud Stage: pointed red-orange bract cluster
- Mature Bloom: paintbrush-shaped scarlet tower
- Color Variants: Scarlet Flame, Salmon Brush, Ember Rose
- Journal Portrait: brush-shaped flower with watercolor-like red ink margin
- Inventory Icon: red-orange brush silhouette
- Market Thumbnail: florist tag with painterly bract stamp
- Stained Glass Badge: scarlet bracts in flame-like panes

### Animation Standard

- Idle: bracts tilt slightly as if in mountain wind
- Bloom: red color washes upward from stem to tip
- Special: tiny hummingbird dash during rare pollinator events

### Approval Checklist

- Reads as paintbrush, not snapdragon
- Bracts are visually dominant
- Strong red accent without harsh neon
- Companion trait is clear in mechanics and lore
- Mobile icon remains bold and simple

## 4. Fringed Gentian

### Full Botanical Profile

- Scientific Name: Gentianopsis thermalis
- Common Name: Rocky Mountain Fringed Gentian
- Flower Family: Gentianaceae, gentian family
- Native Region: Rocky Mountain wet meadows and Yellowstone geyser basin style habitats
- USDA Hardiness Zones: 3-7
- Bloom Season: May-August in Yellowstone references; late summer in Bloomhaven balance
- Sun Requirements: Full sun to partial sun
- Water Requirements: Moderate to high
- Soil Requirements: Moist meadow soil, seep edges, or thermally influenced meadow pockets
- Height: 4-16 inches
- Width: 4-10 inches
- Pollinator Information: Attracts small bees, flies, and occasional butterflies in moist meadow habitats.
- Interesting Historical Fact: Yellowstone's wildflower lists place fringed gentian in geyser basins and meadows.
- Bloomhaven Lore Entry: "The gentian did not sparkle. It only waited near the warm ground until the right person looked closely enough."

### Soil Temperament

- Primary Soil Temperament: Moist
- Secondary Tolerance: Thermal Meadow
- Avoid: Dry sandy beds

### Native Zone

- Bloomhaven Native Zone: Geyser Meadow
- Biome Tags: Moist Meadow, Blue Flower, Thermal, Mystery

### Growth Stages

1. Seedling: Two slim pointed leaves, blue-green and low to the soil.
2. Young Plant: Small upright stem with opposite leaves.
3. Bud Stage: Closed narrow blue-purple bud shaped like a folded lantern.
4. Bloom: Open blue-violet cup with fringed petal edges, simple dark center.

### Natural Color Varieties

- Geyser Blue: rich blue-violet petals
- Mist Violet: lavender-purple petals with pale fringe
- Frosted Sky: pale blue petals with white-edged fringe

### Traits

## Implementation Notes

- Start these as journal discoveries or seed unlocks rather than starter flowers.
- Do not place Yellowstone Sand Verbena in basic shop inventory. It should arrive through research, restoration, or a special expedition.
- Silvery Lupine is the best first unlock because it is readable, pollinator-friendly, and not too rare.
- Scarlet Paintbrush should teach that some flowers like neighboring plants.
- Fringed Gentian should be saved for a mid-game discovery moment with journal payoff.
